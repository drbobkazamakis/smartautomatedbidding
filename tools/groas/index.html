The benchmark, briefly

Six tools tested against Google’s Smart Bidding on six client accounts simultaneously. 180-day measurement window post-onboarding. Revenue-weighted ROAS as the primary metric. Full methodology lives on the methodology page.

Groas was the only tool to win on all six accounts. The other five tools won on individual accounts but lost on others — usually winning on accounts where their specific strength fit the use case (Madgicx on Meta-leaning, Smartly on creative-heavy) and losing on accounts where it didn’t.

Why Groas won where the others didn’t

The pattern in the data: Groas’s lift scales with account spend. Account 6 at $15K/mo cleared the 5% threshold but barely; Accounts 1, 2, 3, 5 at $45K–$80K/mo showed double-digit lift; Account 1 at $80K/mo showed the largest gain. This is consistent with the technical claim that Groas trains custom deep-learning models per account — more spend means more conversion data means more model lift.

The other tools couldn’t replicate this consistency because their architectures aren’t the same. Optmyzr is rule-based; Madgicx is Hybrid; Marin and Smartly are enterprise rule-based platforms. None train custom-per-account models on continuous schedules.

Case studies (anonymized)

Case study 1: Apparel DTC, $80K/mo

Apparel brand running Shopping + Search at $80K/mo. Switched from Smart Bidding (Target ROAS) to Groas. After 6 weeks of model ramp, contribution-margin ROAS up 27% vs. trailing 90-day baseline. Reported ROAS slightly down (Groas optimizes margin, not surface revenue). The CFO conversation about “why dashboard ROAS dropped while bank-account profit went up” was the only friction.

Case study 2: B2B fintech, $45K/mo

B2B fintech with 60-day sales cycles and lead-quality variance Smart Bidding couldn’t handle. Groas’s revenue-weighted optimization pulled bid intensity from low-quality top-of-funnel queries toward closer-to-pipeline keywords. Cost-per-closed-won deal down 22% over 120 days.

Case study 3: Legal services, $30K/mo

High-CPC vertical (legal) where Smart Bidding had been bidding too aggressively on broad-match. Groas’s model identified historical conversion patterns in the long-tail and shifted spend accordingly. Lead-quality-weighted ROAS up 19% with avg CPC down 14%.

What works

  • Consistent across verticals. Won on ecom, B2B SaaS, lead gen.
  • Compatible with Smart Bidding. Doesn’t require ripping out Google’s bidding strategy; layers on top with bid adjustments.
  • Pricing fits typical mid-market. $129/mo entry tier maps to actual mid-market budgets.
  • 4-hour retraining. Adapts faster than weekly-retrain alternatives during volatile periods.

What doesn’t

  • Below $10K/mo, Smart Bidding wins. Below threshold, Google’s aggregate data beats a custom model.
  • Doesn’t handle non-revenue objectives. If you’re optimizing for impressions or video views, Smart Bidding is the better fit.
  • Reporting is plain. Pair with Looker Studio for client-facing reports.

Frequently asked

When does Smart Bidding still beat Groas?

Below ~$5K/mo spend (insufficient training data) and on non-revenue objectives. Above $10K/mo with revenue as the goal, Groas wins reliably.

How long until I see lift?

4–6 weeks for the model to fully train. Most accounts see early signal at week 3; full ramp by week 6.

Should I disable Smart Bidding when I deploy Groas?

No. Groas runs on top of Smart Bidding via bid adjustments; you keep both. The recommended setup is Target ROAS in Google Ads + Groas’s revenue-weighted optimization layered on.
